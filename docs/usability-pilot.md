# Collision Investigator Usability Pilot

Use this protocol after a participant has completed the Guided course. This is curriculum validation, not forensic-tool validation or competency testing. Recruit 3–5 collision investigators with a mix of experience levels. Do not collect names, case information, or prediction answers.

## Opening script

“TrigLab is an educational visualizer, not validated forensic software. We are testing whether the explanations are clear—not testing you. Please think aloud and point out any wording that makes you hesitate.”

## Teach-back prompts

1. In your own words, what does tangent describe?
2. Why does inverse tangent return an angle?
3. Why does `atan2` need both signed components?
4. For the example ΔVx = −18 and ΔVy = 7, identify the quadrant, resultant magnitude, and displayed mathematical direction.
5. What convention warning would you need to resolve before interpreting that direction in an investigation?

Do not correct the participant during the first explanation. Ask “What on the screen led you to that answer?” and record the wording or visual they used.

## Observation notes

For each prompt, record:

- Clear without prompting / clear after prompting / unresolved.
- First point of hesitation.
- Jargon that required explanation.
- Visual or interaction that helped.
- Participant’s suggested wording, if any.

## Exit questions

1. Which module felt most directly useful to your work?
2. Which module felt least necessary or should be optional?
3. Was any statement easy to misread as a case conclusion or a specific PDOF convention?
4. What would you want on the printed Quick Reference during a field or report review?

## Synthesis

After all sessions, group repeated hesitation points. Prioritize changes that appear in two or more sessions, convention language that any participant misinterprets, and terms that repeatedly require facilitator explanation. Re-run the affected teach-back prompt after revising the curriculum.
