# Collision investigator usability pilot

Use this protocol while a participant completes the Guided course for the first time. This is curriculum validation, not forensic-tool validation or competency testing. Recruit 3 to 5 collision investigators with a mix of experience levels. Do not collect names, case information, or prediction answers.

## Success measures

The overhaul succeeds when at least 80% of the course microsteps are completed without the facilitator pointing out where to click, and at least 4 of the 5 teach-back prompts are clear without prompting. Also record whether each participant can always identify the current module, the current step, and the next action.

## Opening script

"TrigLab is an educational visualizer, not validated forensic software. We are testing whether the explanations and controls are clear. We are not testing you. Please think aloud and point out anything that makes you hesitate."

## Unaided walkthrough

Start with course progress reset. Ask the participant to complete all seven modules without an explanation of the interface. The facilitator may remind the participant to think aloud but should not identify a control until a hesitation has been recorded.

For every microstep, record:

- Completed without help / completed after a neutral prompt / required a direct pointer.
- Time from the screen appearing to the first purposeful action.
- The first place the participant looked.
- Any control or label the participant tried before the intended action.
- Whether the participant could state what the screen wanted them to do.

Use the neutral prompt "What do you think this screen is asking you to do?" before giving a direct pointer. Record every direct pointer as facilitator assistance.

## Teach-back prompts

1. In your own words, what does tangent describe?
2. Why does inverse tangent return an angle?
3. Why does `atan2` need both signed components?
4. For the example ΔVx = −18 and ΔVy = 7, identify the quadrant, resultant magnitude, and displayed mathematical direction.
5. What convention warning would you need to resolve before interpreting that direction in an investigation?

Do not correct the participant during the first explanation. Ask "What on the screen led you to that answer?" and record the wording or visual they used.

## Observation notes

For each prompt, record:

- Clear without prompting / clear after prompting / unresolved.
- First point of hesitation.
- Jargon that required explanation.
- Visual or interaction that helped.
- The participant's suggested wording, if any.

## Exit questions

1. Which module felt most directly useful to your work?
2. Which module felt least necessary or should be optional?
3. Was any statement easy to misread as a case conclusion or a specific PDOF convention?
4. What would you want on the printed Quick Reference during a field or report review?

## Synthesis and decision rule

Calculate the unaided completion rate as microsteps completed without help divided by total microsteps attempted. Report the median time to first purposeful action and the number of direct pointers per participant. Group repeated hesitation points. Prioritize changes that appear in two or more sessions, convention language that any participant misinterprets, and terms that repeatedly require facilitator explanation. Re-run the affected walkthrough and teach-back prompt after revising the curriculum.
